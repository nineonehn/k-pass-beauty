Before you book

Questions people actually ask

Is the $9.90 a deposit, or is it taken off the treatment price?

Neither. $9.90 is a flat booking fee — not a deposit and not a prepayment — and nothing is taken off the treatment, because the venue charges you its own published price on the day. We take no cut from the spa.

What the fee buys: the Korean phone call, the confirmed slot, the English voucher, the Korean card, the map links, and a rebooking if the venue cancels on you. You pay it by card or PayPal at the link on the booking form. If we can’t get you in, that same $9.90 is sent back to the same PayPal account the same day.

Do I need a Korean phone number or a Korean card?

No. That’s the whole point — the venue’s booking page asks for both, and we’re the ones who get past it. You need WhatsApp or email, and a debit or credit card (or PayPal) for the $9.90.

Can’t I just book the venue myself?

For a one-chair salon in Yeonnam, a private scrub room in a residential building or a neighbourhood head spa, booking runs through KakaoTalk, a phone call or a Korean-only app. Every one of those wants a Korean number, and the conversation happens in Korean — that is where most guests stop and give up on the shop.

Our job is narrow: we get that specific shop on the phone, in Korean, ask what the venue will ask you anyway — room type, pressure, tattoos, pregnancy, allergies — and come back with a confirmed time or your money. You send one link; the call is ours to make.

All I have is an Instagram handle. Is that enough?

Yes. An Instagram profile, a Naver Map pin, a Kakao Map link, a screenshot of the post, or just the shop’s name and neighbourhood — any one of those is enough to start. We find the exact branch, check that they take foreign guests, and call them.

If it turns out to be the wrong branch, fully booked, or a shop that won’t take foreign guests, the $9.90 comes back in full and we tell you the reason — not a maybe.

I haven’t picked a shop yet — can you choose one for me?

Yes. On the form choose “Not sure yet — recommend a salon for me”, then the area you’d like: Hongdae · Sinchon · Yeonnam, Myeongdong · Euljiro · Jongno, Seongsu · Konkuk Univ., Gangnam · Apgujeong · Hannam, or “anywhere convenient — near my hotel is fine”. Add your budget or the treatment you’re after if you have one.

We come back with two or three verified foreigner-friendly shops in that area and book the one that fits — the $9.90 fee stays the same, and the treatment is paid to the shop at its own price. If none of them can take you at one of your three times, we keep looking or refund the fee in full.

I have tattoos. Can I still go?

Many Korean bathhouses and scrub rooms restrict tattoos, and it varies by venue. We ask the specific venue before booking and tell you the real answer — including “no”. We never promise access we haven’t confirmed.

Will I be naked? Who is in the room?

It depends on the venue: some scrub rooms are one-person and private, others are on a shared floor, and some provide disposable underwear. We ask about the room type, what you wear, and whether the therapist is the same sex as you, and we put their answer in the voucher. We never guess.

Can I choose how hard the scrub is?

Yes — soft, medium or “시원하게” (strong). You pick it on the form, it goes on the therapist’s card in Korean, and you can still change your mind on the day.

What if I’m on my period, pregnant, or have allergies?

Tell us on the form. Many venues ask guests to use a tampon or menstrual cup, and some decline guests who are menstruating. We confirm before booking so you aren’t turned away at the counter. Allergies and pregnancy go to the therapist in Korean.

What if I need to cancel or move my booking?

Before a slot is confirmed: message us and we stop working the request. No venue is booked, and the $9.90 is refunded in full — you don’t have to give a reason.

After it’s confirmed: the venue’s own cancellation window applies, and it differs from venue to venue — usually 24 hours, sometimes same-day. We write the rule the venue actually gave us into your voucher, so you read it before the appointment. Cancelling inside that window, or not turning up, can forfeit the deposit you paid the venue; our $9.90 is the separate fee, not their deposit.

Moving the time: send us the new time and we ask the venue. We come back with their answer before you agree to anything — if they charge for the change, we say the amount first.

Do you come with me?

No. We don’t escort or interpret in person — you go alone with the voucher and the Korean card, and the staff read it in one second. If something goes wrong on site, message us and we call the venue for you.

What exactly do I show at the counter?

Your English voucher and the Korean card attached to it. The card states who you are, the time, the treatment, the pressure you chose and anything the therapist has to know — in Korean, in the venue’s own terms, so the staff read it in a second. You don’t have to explain anything with a translation app.

The voucher also carries the Korean address to hand a taxi driver, plus Naver and Kakao Map links that open on your phone. If the venue can’t find your booking, message us — we call them in Korean and sort it out while you wait.

How fast is it, really?

You hear from us within 2 hours between 09:00 and 22:00 KST, seven days a week. The confirmed voucher comes in 2–4 hours, and it needs all three of your time options. Requests sent overnight are answered by 11:00.

What if the venue is fully booked?

You get 100% of the $9.90 back the same day, or we keep trying another venue if you’d rather. There’s no “we’ll see what we can do” middle state.

Is this a spa? Do you guarantee results?

We’re a booking desk, not a spa, a clinic or a medical service. We describe what happens at the venue, in their words — no detox claims, no “cures”, no before-and-after promises. If you want medical treatment, this is the wrong service and we’d rather say so.
